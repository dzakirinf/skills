# IA grounding

The frameworks the page shapes echo. These are defaults — if the team keeps its own information-architecture sources, prefer those and reconcile the vocabulary.

- **Explanation vs. reference** (Diátaxis). A hub/overview is *understanding-oriented* explanation; a content leaf often leans reference. Keep them distinct — don't drop a tutorial or a config dump into an overview, and don't bury the explanation under a reference table.
- **Information scent & hub-and-spoke** (Nielsen Norman Group). The hub page is the spoke-center; every child link must telegraph what the reader gains by following it. A bare bold page-name carries no scent and is a dead end.
- **Every page is page one** (Mark Baker). Assume readers arrive mid-cluster from search, not from the top. Each page self-orients (a lead that says what it is) and links onward (up to its parent, across to siblings, down to its children).
- **Inverted pyramid.** Lead with the one-sentence definition; expand below. The reader who stops after the panel should still have the gist.
